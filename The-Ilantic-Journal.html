The Ilantic JournalIn formation: for founding partners

A journal for the theory that makes other work possible.

The Ilantic Journal publishes theoretical research programs rather than papers, tests them through adversarial review published in full, and accounts for what they produced five and ten years later.

Where the paper falls short

The paper suits most of science. It suits less well the work that changes what a field is able to ask.

The unit is wrong

A bounded argument cannot carry a framework whose value lies in the questions it makes possible, so the architecture disappears into the results built on it.

Priority is hard to secure

Long theoretical work pushes authors to publish early and incomplete in order to claim the idea as theirs.

No one keeps the account

Journals rarely return to ask what a published idea went on to build, or whether its predictions held.

Three commitments

We know of no journal that combines all three.

The theoretical research program

A program must name the wall the field could not pass, build the path beyond it, state what follows and what it displaces. Authors first submit a Program Architecture Document. Its acceptance fixes a public date of theoretical priority.

Adversarial review, published in full

Independent panels are asked for one thing: the flaw that would bring the program down. Every attack attempted, and why it failed, is published beside the program.

The Living Record

Formal reassessments at five and ten years account for the research lines a program opened, where its predictions held, and which fields it reached.

What is settled and what is open

The vision is still being shaped. We prefer to show where it is firm and where we want partners’ judgment.

Settled

  • The program, not the paper, is the unit of publication.
  • Every decision asks one question: does this change what the field is able to ask?
  • Review is adversarial, and its full record is public.
  • Two tracks, one standard: established programs with a five-year record, and advancing programs assessed on architecture.
  • Incremental programs register their architecture first and keep binding deadlines.

Open

  • Funding model and fees. Undecided. Any model should avoid tying revenue to acceptance decisions.
  • Initial scope. Which one or two fields to begin with.
  • Panels. Composition, recruitment, and compensation.
  • AI tools. Their role in review and reassessment.
  • Governance. How editorial independence is protected.

A likely sequence

Pilot

Apply the method to established programs with a five-year record, where the outcome can already be checked.

Narrow opening

Open to new programs in one or two fields, with a small founding panel.

Wider scope

Extend field by field as the published record supports it.

An indicative order only. No dates have been set.

How partners can take part

Partners help settle the open questions above and are recognized as founders of the journal.

Founding reviewers

Researchers willing to serve on early adversarial panels.

Advisors

Voices on scope, criteria, and governance.

Funders

Support for a review process that is costly by design.

Pilot hosts

Institutions able to host the first established-program reviews.

Origin
